    vmctx: the lifecycle design -- a context's service outlives everything that made it
    
    Three rules, one change. The tree is the service lifetime: vmremote's
    main waits for every forked context's service to drain, bounded by
    liveness rather than a clock, with the counter-leak case the old
    two-second bound existed for detected by audit instead. A dead monitor
    is a missing monitor: the kernel publishes the event and waits for a
    successor under the fault deadline instead of improvising an ACT_SELF
    that is an unrecoverable -14 for a redirected context -- and each forked
    context's monitor is handed from the thread that spawned it to the
    thread that services it, so a guest enters exactly when its own watcher
    is in place and can never outrun or outlive it. Claims and transits die
    with their wire: a service that breaks out settles the claim it wrote,
    and unacked single-page transits are settled THEIRS when the connection
    that committed them closes.
    
    Measured on kernel #179: nc1 9/9 -- first passes ever, the orphaned
    server survives its parent; sort 9/9 against a 2-in-12 history; and
    lddnames 6/6, which rewrites a diagnosis -- the "argv-stale exec hole"
    that four kernel wipe builds chased was the clone-window race, closed by
    the ordering alone. All three graduate from KNOWN_GAPS to strict.
    
    Gates: suite 120/0 x3, cross 40/0, and the breadth harness at
    66 pass / 0 fail -- the first zero-failure full campaign on record.
    Remaining known gaps: gpgsym (a live-connection claim storm, the last
    coat of the old wall) and the two ptrace-semantics cases.
